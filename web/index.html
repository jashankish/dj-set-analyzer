        How it works

        1. Drop the recording

          A phone recording from the floor works. So does a clean export from your DJ software. Sets of two hours or more are fine on a laptop.

        2. Your browser listens

          Essentia finds the beats, tempo and key of every moment. The Discogs-EffNet model hears the style and mood. It then works out where each track starts, how each mix was done, and where the drops land. Expect about a minute per hour of audio on a recent laptop.

        3. Replay and explore

          Play the highlights reel, read the review, open any transition and loop it, see how the set was mixed and how its mood moved, and take its sound to Spotify as a playlist prompt. Name the tracks you know. Your sets stay saved in this browser for next time.

        Questions

        Is my audio uploaded anywhere?
        No. Decoding and analysis happen on your device in WebAssembly. The page downloads its analysis models once (about 35 MB, then cached); your audio and results are never sent anywhere.
        Why are tracks called “ID”?
        Naming songs needs Shazam's fingerprint database, and browsers aren't allowed to ask it directly. Set Replay still finds where each track starts and how it was mixed, and you can double-click any ID to name it. For automatic names, on your computer and Set Replay will ask Shazam through it.
        Can it make a playlist from my set?
        Yes. Each set gets a prompt for Spotify's Prompted Playlist (or YouTube Music, Apple Music, Deezer or any AI chat) written from what Set Replay measured: the genres it trusts, the tempo, the mood and its route through the set, the artists and songs to start from, and what to leave out. Switch keywords on and off, copy it, and paste it into the app. With a Spotify app's Client ID it can also save the set's named tracks to your Spotify as a playlist.
        Can I drop in a video?
        Yes: an MP4 or MOV of the set, straight from a phone. Only its soundtrack is taken out (without re-encoding and without loading the video into memory), analyzed and kept, so an hour of video costs about 60 MB in your browser.
        How accurate is it?
        Tempo, key and swap points are reliable. Mix-in and mix-out are estimates to within a few seconds. Style labels are best read as relative: “harder than the last track” rather than a definitive genre.
        Which browsers work?
        Recent Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari on a laptop or desktop. Very long sets need a few gigabytes of free memory while they're being analyzed.
